Preamble
An Act to provide for measures to protect personal data by treating an individual's personal data as belonging
to that individual, and to make provisions concerning the lawful processing of such data with the individual's
consent and for matters ancillary and connected thereto.
WHEREAS it is expedient and necessary to provide for the lawful processing of an individual's personal data
with the individual's consent, treating such personal data as belonging to that individual; and
WHEREAS, in processing personal data, it is necessary to ensure the fundamental rights of individuals,
particularly privacy, integrity, protection and security of data, together with fairness and interoperability, and
to encourage and protect innovation for the development of the digital economy; and
WHEREAS it is necessary to ensure the discharge of responsibilities entrusted to data-fiduciaries and
processors and to provide remedies by establishing arrangements for lawful processing and audit;
NOW, THEREFORE, it is hereby enacted as follows:
CHAPTER I — PRELIMINARY
1. Short title, application and commencement
(1) This Act shall be called the Personal Data Protection Act, 2026.
(2) This Act shall apply to any person, data-fiduciary, processor, person associated with processing, or other
person performing a duty or function under this Act, who—
• (a) is a citizen of Bangladesh, resides or ordinarily resides in Bangladesh, works in Bangladesh, or is
temporarily present in Bangladesh for work or activity;
• (b) processes personal data within Bangladesh, other than merely transferring personal data through
Bangladesh; or
• (c) processes personal data outside Bangladesh in connection with providing goods or services to, or
monitoring or profiling, a data subject located in Bangladesh.
(3) Except for section 23 and sections 31 to 35, this Act shall be deemed to have come into force on 6
November 2025. Section 23 and sections 31 to 35 shall come into force on such date as the Government may
appoint by notification in the Official Gazette after eighteen months following issuance of this Act.

(2) "Data-fiduciary" means a person who, individually or jointly, processes personal data for a specific
purpose, supervises it for that purpose, or authorizes another person to process personal data for that purpose.
(3) "Data subject" means a natural person to whom personal data relates, whether identified or identifiable and
whether living or deceased.
(4) "Authority" means the National Data Management Authority established under section 8 of the National
Data Management Act, 2026.
(5) "Significant data-fiduciary" means a data-fiduciary determined by regulation on the basis of potential
impact on State sovereignty; the volume or financial concentration of processed data; risks to data-subject
rights; and potential threats to national security, public order, public safety, economic order and public health.
(6) "Pseudonymized Data" means processed personal data from which the data subject cannot be identified
without using additional information stored separately.
(7) "Genetic Data" means data concerning hereditary or ancestral genetic characteristics of an individual that
provides information about behavioral characteristics, physiological condition or health, obtained from
analysis of the individual's biological sample or bodily fluid.
(8) "Retention" means continuous storage of data in a manner that allows the data subject to be identified and
the original purpose of collection continues to exist, or the data is required for lawful operation or processing.
(9) "Auditor" means an independent data auditor referred to in section 21(1).
(10) "Processing" means any operation performed on personal data, whether automated or not, including
collection, recording, organization, structuring, storage, retention, transfer, adaptation or alteration, retrieval,
use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination, alignment or combination, restriction, destruction or erasure.
(11) "Processor" means a person who processes personal data on behalf of a data-fiduciary.
(12) "Regulation" means a regulation made under this Act.
(13) "Profiling" means automated processing of personal information relating to an individual for evaluating,
analyzing or predicting aspects concerning performance at work, economic situation, health, personal
preferences, interests, reliability, behavior, habits, location or movements.
(14) "Biometric Data" means personal data created through measurement or technical processing of physical,
physiological or behavioral characteristics capable of identifying a specific individual or confirming identity,
including DNA, blood group, fingerprint, facial image, iris scan, voiceprint and gait pattern.
(15) "Rules" means rules made under this Act.
(16) "Person" means, for a data subject, any natural person; and for a data-fiduciary or processor, a legal
person.
(17) "Personal Data" means information or data relating to an individual, including name, parents' names,
identification number, mobile number, financial data identifying an individual, location-identification data or
similar online identifiers, and physical, physiological, genetic, biometric, psychological or financial data, or
any element prescribed by regulation that assists in identifying an individual.
(18) "Personal Data Breach" means a breach of personal-data security resulting in unauthorized access to, or
unlawful transfer, disclosure or alteration of, personal data processed under this Act, or an accident,
destruction, loss or intrusion arising from absence of necessary means for proper processing and storage.
(19) "Child" means a person under eighteen years of age or such other age as the Government may prescribe.

3. Overriding effect of the Act
Notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in any other law for the time being in force, the provisions
of this Act shall have effect.
4. Extraterritorial application
(1) If a citizen of Bangladesh violates this Act outside Bangladesh in circumstances where the act would be
punishable under this Act if committed in Bangladesh, this Act shall apply as if the violation had been
committed in Bangladesh.
(2) If any person violates this Act from outside Bangladesh in relation to Bangladesh, this Act shall apply as if
the entire process of the violation had occurred in Bangladesh.
(3) If any person violates this Act from within Bangladesh in relation to outside Bangladesh, this Act shall
apply as if the entire process of the violation had occurred in Bangladesh.
CHAPTER II — COLLECTION AND PROCESSING OF PERSONAL DATA
5. Lawful basis for processing personal data
(1) A data-fiduciary or processor may process a data subject's personal data with consent, in accordance with
this Act and regulations made under it.
(2) Consent shall be voluntary, specific, clear and revocable. The data subject shall be informed of the purpose
of processing, retention period, transfer and withdrawal procedure.
(3) Without consent, a data-fiduciary may process data where a lawful basis or legitimate interest exists,
subject to regulations and limitations based on benefit, necessity, proportionality and purpose, including—
• performance of a contract to which the data subject is a party;
• steps requested by the data subject for entering into a contract;
• necessity for establishing a legal right or bringing or defending a legal proceeding;
• protection of vital interests relating to life or health;
• implementation of legal rights concerning employment, labour rights or social security;
• data voluntarily made public by the data subject; or
• where unreasonable withholding of consent is likely to cause harm to another person.

(4) The burden of proving that consent was properly obtained rests on the data-fiduciary.
6. Conditions for further processing
A data-fiduciary may, where consistent with the original purpose, further process a data subject's personal data
where necessary in accordance with this Act, rules or regulations.
7. Conditions for processing sensitive personal data
Subject to sections 5 and 6, a data-fiduciary may process sensitive personal data where—
• specific consent of the data subject is obtained;
• the data subject enters into a contract as a party;
• processing is required under employment or social-security law in the course of the data-fiduciary's duties;
• a healthcare professional performs medical duties, including urgent care relating to risk to life or health;
• a duty imposed by or under law is performed; or
• the data subject voluntarily makes the personal data public.
8. Processing by a processor
(1) A data-fiduciary may appoint or otherwise engage a processor to process personal data on its behalf for
activities relating to provision of goods or services under a lawful contract.
(2) Processing by a processor on behalf of a data-fiduciary shall be deemed processing by the data-fiduciary,
which remains responsible under this Act.
(3) A data-fiduciary shall take reasonable measures to ensure that processors comply with this Act and
regulations, and shall remain responsible for the processor's acts.
9. Children and persons unable to give consent
(1) A data-fiduciary may collect or process personal data of a child or person unable to give consent with
consent of a parent, lawful guardian or authorized decision-maker, in the prescribed manner.
(2) Processing shall protect the rights and interests of the child or person unable to give consent.
(3) Such consent remains valid until the child reaches eighteen years or the person acquires capacity to
consent, as applicable.
CHAPTER III — RIGHTS OF DATA SUBJECTS
10. General conditions for exercising rights
(1) A data subject may exercise rights under this Chapter by written application to the data-fiduciary.
(2) Acknowledgment, grounds for refusal, procedures and related matters shall be prescribed by regulations.
(3) On receiving a request, the data-fiduciary shall verify relevant data sensitivity and fraud risk, take
appropriate action, retain records for audit where applicable, and inform the data subject.
(4) These rights are universal, inherent, non-transferable and inviolable and cannot be cancelled or derogated
from by contract or notice.
11. Right of access and portability
(1) A data subject shall have access to data processed by the data-fiduciary.

(2) On request, the data-fiduciary shall provide collected and processed personal data in the prescribed manner
and, where applicable, may directly transfer it to another data-fiduciary using a Federated Interoperable
Ecosystem.
(3) A copy shall be provided in concise and intelligible form and, as prescribed, shall include a summary of the
data, actions, purpose, type, recipients, retention, source, cross-border safeguards, and the logic and
significance of automated decisions.
(4) The copy shall include information identifying persons, data-fiduciaries or processors with whom the data
was shared.
(5) If disclosure may threaten national security, law and order, or third-party rights, the data-fiduciary shall
notify the Authority in writing and act according to its decision.
(6) Request frequency, receipt, response and disposal periods and other matters shall be prescribed.
Explanation: A Federated Interoperable Ecosystem is a process in which multiple operators exchange the
minimum necessary information securely and in a standardized manner on mutual request while retaining
control, responsibility and accountability over their respective data.
12. Right to rectification, updating and completion
(1) A data subject may, having regard to the purpose of processing, require correction of inaccurate or
misleading data, completion of incomplete data, and rectification or updating of data retained for processing.
(2) If the data-fiduciary refuses, it shall provide written reasonable grounds.
(3) If the data subject is not satisfied, the data subject may request that the data be marked as disputed and that
the Authority be notified.
(4) If data is corrected, completed or updated, the data-fiduciary shall notify the data subject and all relevant
persons within thirty days.
(5) Procedures for requests and related matters shall be prescribed by regulations.
13. Withdrawal of consent, objection to processing and consequences
(1) A data subject may at any time wholly or partially withdraw consent, through the simplified procedure
prescribed by regulations, concerning retention of personal data, processing of personal data, or automated
decisions relating to personal data.
(2) At the data subject's request, the data-fiduciary shall erase relevant personal data if the original purpose no
longer exists; consent is withdrawn; the data subject objects under this Act; the data was unlawfully processed;
or erasure is necessary for a legal obligation or prescribed condition.
(3) Erasure may be refused where the data concerns an identifier usable to identify a person while consent
remains valid; is designated for retention or archival purposes; is retained for legal compliance; is confidential
personal data; or is restricted personal data.
(4) Withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing carried out while consent was valid.
(5) Where the data subject believes processing may cause harm, the data subject may communicate an
intention to withdraw and the data-fiduciary shall stop the processing while keeping the data subject informed.
(6) The data-fiduciary shall not take automated processing, withdrawal, transfer or other action under this Act
without informing the data subject, as applicable.

14. System-wide propagation
(1) For an approved request under section 12 or 13, the primary data-fiduciary shall, where applicable, follow
the prescribed system-wide propagation procedure.
(2) The Authority shall determine an order of precedence among data-field sources; the highest-priority source
shall be the primary source and relevant records of other data-fiduciaries or processors shall be made
consistent with it.
(3) The primary data-fiduciary shall verify and implement updates or deletion in its own system and notify the
Authority, in the prescribed manner, of changed fields and retention warnings.
(4) The Authority shall automatically direct all relevant secondary data-fiduciaries and processors to
implement the approved change without delay and shall record each change and acknowledgment in an
immutable ledger, blockchain or equivalent technology.
(5) Once a primary source is determined according to the order of accuracy, its information shall be used
consistently in relevant government and private registers, licences, financial transactions, ownership records
and other applicable contexts.
(6) System-wide propagation also applies to mirrors, caches, backups, disaster-recovery systems, test
environments and migration datasets.
(7) Propagation steps shall be completed within the prescribed period and, where applicable, recorded in an
immutable ledger and reported to the Authority.
(8) Where erasure may distort a record, only public-interest data or archival/scientific/historical records
specified by the Authority may be exempted from erasure; reasons must be stated and the refusal may be
appealed.
Explanation: Where the same information, such as a current address, exists in several registers and differs, the
primary source shall be determined by considering the original collection method and accuracy, data quality
and update history, source reliability and verifiability, and automated or semi-automated capability. The most
accurate and authoritative register becomes the Primary Source of Truth, and other registers shall be updated
accordingly. For example, if a passport register is the primary source for current address, other government
registers shall be updated; for a person without a passport, the next source in the order of accuracy, such as
NID, local-government registration, utility bill, employment, health or police information, shall be used.
CHAPTER IV — DUTIES AND RESPONSIBILITIES
15. Accountability and transparency
(1) A data-fiduciary or processor is responsible for performing its duties under this Act.
(2) It shall process personal data transparently and inform the data subject, in relation to important processing
activities, of the categories and collection method; purposes; categories creating risk of harm; rights-exercise
procedure and contact details; complaint information; applicable transfers; the data-fiduciary's identity and
contact method; and other prescribed matters.
(3) Forms and procedures for providing such information shall be prescribed.
(4) Where compliance involves disproportionate effort or expense, the data-fiduciary or processor shall take
measures determined by the Authority.

16. Confidentiality
Subject to this Act, without the data subject's consent personal data shall not be disclosed for a purpose other
than the purpose for which it was collected.
17. Standards for protection and security
(1) Every data-fiduciary and processor shall implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to
protect security, accuracy and confidentiality and prevent accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, misuse,
alteration, unauthorized disclosure or access.
(2) Measures shall consider data volume and sensitivity; potential harm to the data subject; scope of
processing; retention period; and availability and probable cost of technologies and measures.
(3) Measures shall include pseudonymization, encryption,
security/integrity/confidentiality/availability/resilience of processing systems, timely recovery after physical or
technical incidents, periodic risk assessment, regular testing/evaluation/audit, and regular updating and
introduction of measures.
(4) The Authority may prescribe standards for such technical and organizational measures.
(5) The processor is responsible for complying with the prescribed security standards.
18. Retention conditions
(1) A data-fiduciary shall not retain personal data beyond the period prescribed for the purpose for which it
was processed.
(2) Where there is no contrary legal obligation, data may be retained beyond that period for public interest,
scientific or historical research or statistical purposes until technical and administrative measures for
protecting data-subject rights are implemented.
19. Record keeping
(1) Subject to section 18, all records relating to personal data processed by a data-fiduciary shall be properly
retained in a register for at least five years unless another law provides otherwise.
(2) Records concerning processing, retention, structuring, subtraction, storage, adaptation, modification,
portability and related matters shall be kept up to date in the prescribed manner.
20. Personal Data Protection Breach
(1) If a breach may cause significant harm to a data subject, the data-fiduciary shall notify the Authority in the
form, manner and time prescribed by regulations.
(2) The Authority shall consider the nature of the breach; affected categories and approximate numbers and
relevant records; the data-fiduciary's contact address; and measures taken or to be taken to address the breach
and reduce adverse effects.
21. Data audit
(1) Classes of data-fiduciaries prescribed by regulation shall arrange audits through an independent auditor
authorized by the Authority.
(2) The auditor shall assess compliance with this Act and regulations.
(3) Auditor qualifications and audit procedures shall be prescribed.

(4) The Authority may constitute an audit panel of persons experienced in ICT, computer systems, personal

(5) If processing may be harmful to a data subject, the Authority may direct an audit by an appointed auditor

22. Data protection plan

concerning misuse or inefficient handling of sensitive personal data within the prescribed period.
(4) The Chief Data Officer shall perform duties having regard to the purpose, nature, scope and context of
overall personal-data processing.
CHAPTER V — EXEMPTIONS
24. Exemptions
(1) Consent shall not be required where processing is necessary for national security, defense, public order or
public interest; compliance with a lawful court order; prevention, detection, investigation or prosecution of
offences; prevention or detection of tax evasion; public health or medical care; investigation of misuse of
public funds; personal, recreational or household activities of the data subject; statistics or scientific/historical
research; public-interest publication, journalism, archival education, artistic work or literary composition; or
other related prescribed matters.
(2) Exemption from consent does not exempt processing from provisions applicable to processing, storage,
retention, disclosure and related activities.
(3) These provisions apply to data-fiduciaries and any person or organization involved in such processing.
CHAPTER VI — FUNCTIONS AND POWERS OF THE AUTHORITY
25. Functions of the Authority
• ensure proper implementation of this Act;
• uphold data-subject rights and prevent and remedy violations and breaches;
• ensure management weaknesses of data-fiduciaries/processors do not harm data-subject interests;
• ensure confidentiality, security, fairness and interoperability of processed data;
• create public awareness of the Act;
• prepare guidelines for efficient processing activities;
• support safe use of personal data for research, development and innovation for economic development;
• coordinate with Chief Data Officers of ministries, divisions, public/private and domestic/foreign
organizations for implementation; and

26. Powers of the Authority
• issue directions to data-fiduciaries and processors concerning processing;
• order production of necessary personal data from data-fiduciaries, processors or authorized

• issue notices concerning alleged violations;

27. Standard Operating Procedure
(1) With prior Government approval and subject to this Act, rules and regulations, the Authority shall
formulate Standard Operating Procedures concerning personal-data processing and related matters.
(2) SOPs may cover consent conditions; exercise of rights; processing matters; quality of processing and
retention; notice forms and requirements; portability; transparency and accountability standards;
pseudonymized-data processing; destruction, erasure and deletion; data-protection impact assessments;
transfers outside Bangladesh; and other necessary matters.
28. Power to issue directions
(1) Subject to this Act, rules and regulations, the Authority may direct a data-fiduciary or person responsible
for processing, storage or transfer to provide personal data or take other necessary action. Compliance is
mandatory.
(2) The Authority may specify a deadline for compliance.
CHAPTER VII — CLASSIFICATION, STORAGE AND TRANSFER
29. Classification of personal data
(1) Having regard to the Schedule, the Government may classify personal data as public/open, internal,
confidential or restricted.
(2) The Government may, in consultation with the Authority, amend the Schedule.
(3) Classified personal data may be transferred abroad where the data subject consents; where the transfer
concerns goods/services under a contract to which the data subject is a party; or, with consent, where it
concerns business, education, emigration, immigration or similar interests.
(4) Transfer is permitted only to places or countries having suitable technology and equipment for personal-
data storage as prescribed by regulation.
(5) The Government may, by Gazette notification, determine a fee or charge on annual business or commercial
profit arising from use of Bangladeshi citizens' personal data.

CHAPTER VIII — COMPLAINTS, ADMINISTRATIVE FINES AND APPEALS
31. Filing of complaints
Where a data subject or any person has reason to believe that a data-fiduciary or processor has violated a data-
subject right or acted in violation of this Act, the data subject or person may file a complaint with the
Authority within the time and in the manner prescribed by regulations.
32. Administrative fine for violation of data-subject rights
(1) Failure by a data-fiduciary or processor to comply with a data-subject right is a violation and may attract an
administrative fine of up to BDT 2,500,000.
(2) For a significant data-fiduciary, the maximum administrative fine under subsection (1) is BDT 5,000,000.
(3) For a second or repeated violation, the Authority may impose an additional fine.
33. Failure to provide adequate protection and security
Failure by a data-fiduciary or processor to provide the protection and security required by this Act, rules,
regulations or the Standard Operating Procedure constitutes negligence in performing its responsibilities and
may attract an administrative fine of up to BDT 2,500,000.
34. Matters considered in determining an administrative fine
• nature, extent, seriousness, duration and recurrence of the failure;
• possible harm to the data subject and possible benefit to the data-fiduciary or processor;
• prompt notification to the Authority of measures taken concerning the violation;
• measures taken after becoming aware of the violation; and
• other matters prescribed by the Authority.
35. Compensation
(1) In disposing of a complaint under sections 32 and 33, the Authority may, on its own discretion or on the
data subject's request, determine compensation, which shall be additional to the administrative fine.
(2) The Authority shall ensure payment of the compensation to the data subject in accordance with the
prescribed procedure.

36. Company violations

CHAPTER IX — MISCELLANEOUS
38. Government's power to issue directions
(1) For sovereignty and integrity of Bangladesh, national security, friendly relations with foreign states or
public order, the Government may from time to time issue directions to the Authority.
(2) The Authority shall comply with such directions while performing its duties, without prejudice to the other
provisions of this Act.
39. Reports, etc.
The Government may require the Authority to submit reports or statements concerning matters carried out
under this Act, and the Authority shall provide them.
40. Personal data processed before commencement
Where a data-fiduciary processed personal data from a data subject or third party before commencement of
this Act, processing may continue until the original purpose changes; if the purpose changes, prior consent to
the changed purpose shall be obtained in accordance with regulations.
41. Power to make rules
(1) The Government may, by Gazette notification, make rules to carry out the purposes of this Act.
(2) To finalize draft rules, the Government shall publish the draft on its website for stakeholder/public
comments within a specified period and publish it in at least one widely circulated Bangla and one widely
circulated English daily newspaper.
(3) After the comment period, the Government shall review comments, make necessary amendments and
publish the rules in the Official Gazette.
42. Power to make regulations
(1) The Authority may, by Gazette notification, make regulations on matters not included in the rules for
carrying out this Act.
(2) Draft regulations shall be published on the Authority's website for stakeholder/public comments and in at
least one widely circulated Bangla and one widely circulated English daily newspaper.
(3) After the comment period, the Authority shall review comments, make necessary amendments, obtain
Government approval and publish the regulations in the Official Gazette.

SCHEDULE
[See section 29]
A. Public or open personal data
Personal data made lawfully accessible to the public through government publications, open platforms,
registers or similar legal means, or by a data subject. This includes data intentionally disclosed for public
interest, public services, research or government transparency where responsible processing presents no
reasonable risk of harm.
B. Internal personal data
Personal data processed and retained for lawful internal operations of an organization or entity for operational,
administrative, human-resources or customer-service purposes. It is not open to the public and shall be
protected from unauthorized access or disclosure. It may include employment details, contact data and service-
use history and may include sensitive personal data where processed strictly in an internal context under legal
safeguards.
C. Confidential personal data
Personal data including sensitive personal data defined by this Act which, if disclosed, shared or processed
without adequate safeguards, may cause significant harm, discrimination or privacy infringement. It includes
financial, health, biometric and real-time-location data and any other data prescribed as sensitive or
confidential that can specifically identify the individual.
D. Restricted personal data
Personal data having potential impact on national security, public order, defense, critical infrastructure, or
fundamental rights and freedoms, for which processing, transfer or access is subject to the strictest controls. It
may include classified datasets, critical health- or security-related data, or other personal data identified as
restricted by the Authority or Government.
